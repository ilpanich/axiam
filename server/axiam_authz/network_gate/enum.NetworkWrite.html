SegmentUpdate

Rewrite a segment’s envelope (network:segments_write at the node).

A change of vlan_id, rank or enabled on a segment that has a reply profile re-places every device that may join it, so it also needs network:grant at the segment (H2:placement) and, on a quarantine segment, at /network/quarantine/<site> (H5:placement). filter_ids alone needs network:segments_write only. A replace cleared without the grant carries a frozen token, so the write re-checks the profile inside its own transaction (R110W6-03).

The new envelope.

SegmentDelete

Delete a segment (network:segments_write at the node).

A segment that has a reply profile takes its placed devices with it, so its delete also needs network:grant at the segment (H2:placement), on a quarantine segment at /network/quarantine/<site> too (H5:placement), then H6 (the decision of 2026-10-16). A delete cleared without the grant (no profile on the gate’s read) carries a frozen token, so the write re-checks the profile inside its own transaction.

PolicyWrite

Write the tenant’s network policy: the Session-Timeout cap (network.session_timeout_max_secs, §3.4).

Needs [NETWORK_SEGMENTS_WRITE] at the /network root (the maintainer’s correction of 2026-10-15, plan §13.6: network:admin is administrative login to a segment’s NAS devices and is not grantable, so it cannot guard this), the writer’s own, uncached, deny-override in force. A grant at a site below the root does not count; a deny at the root refuses. A raise asks nothing more than a lowering does, and the reasoning is the point of this variant:

  • H3 reads the cap as a profile writer’s envelope, so a raise widens what a writer holding network:profiles_write and network:grant may later set. It changes no stored profile and no reply already rendered: a stored session_timeout_secs was within the cap in force when it was written, and a read renders min(stored, cap), so a raise to any value cannot render more than some writer once chose. (A raise after a lowering restores such a value; it was a writer’s, within the cap then.)
  • The cap is the tenant-wide axis of the envelope, beside the per-segment one (network:segments_write). The note splits those duties from profile writing on purpose (§5.2 H3: “a tenant can split the two duties between two people”), so asking the raiser to hold network:profiles_write or network:grant as well would undo the split. Writing the cap is an envelope duty, and envelopes are network:segments_write’s; a holder of network:profiles_write alone cannot write it.
  • H6’s reading (a subject placeable on a segment may not shape what joining gives) has no subject to bite on today: the route is human-only and a user is not a device until D6 binds certificates to users, which reopens §5.3 with §4.2 rule 2.

The raise is therefore distinguished only in the audit row (network_policy.raised), not by an extra check.

Fields

§session_timeout_max_secs: u32

The cap the write would store.
