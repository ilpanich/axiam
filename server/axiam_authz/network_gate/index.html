The authorization function of the network write surface (RADIUS Live profile, W6.2; claude_dev/radius-reply-profile-design.md §5.1–§5.3).

§What it decides

One function, authorize_network_write, answers for every write that can create or change who may be placed on a network segment, and what being placed there gives:

  • a reply-profile write, against H1–H6 of §5.2, after refusing an administrative Service-Type outright (§3.3; a 400, #700);
  • a segment write (network:segments_write at the parent or the node; a replace that changes vlan_id, rank or enabled of a segment that has a profile, or a delete of such a segment, also needs network:grant there, and on a quarantine segment at its quarantine site — H2 and H5 applied to the envelope, correction R110W6-03 of the note and the decision of 2026-10-16: every write that moves or removes placed devices needs the placing right);
  • a role assignment, unassignment, permission grant or revoke, role update or delete that touches a network-sensitive role, against the §5.3 table and the three write-time rules of §4.2, and against H6 where the write would widen the writer’s own placement (a join it receives, a deny it sheds; correction R110W6-01 of the note);
  • a certificate bind to a service account, against §5.3’s binding rows and H6 (correction R101W6-03 of the note);
  • a write of the tenant’s network policy, the Session-Timeout cap that H3 reads as the envelope (network:segments_write at /network; see NetworkWrite::PolicyWrite for why a raise asks no more than a lowering);
  • a write of the generic surfaces that could reach into the reserved namespace: a permission whose action is in network: (create, rename, delete), and a resource of a reserved network_* type, or under one, or re-parented into or out of the subtree (§4.1).

Every “holds” is a WriteTimeAccess::check_at_write_time — the engine’s own evaluation, uncached, deny-override in full force, with the writer’s subject scope, so an organization-level writer carries only its global grants (§2). A deny at the node or an ancestor is “does not hold”.

§What it returns

A GateClearance or a GateRefusal. A refusal carries the HTTP class (403 for a permission the writer lacks, 400 for a write no permission makes legal), the internal rule that refused (H2, 4.2:rule-2, …), the permission and node a 403 names, and the before and after values the audit row records (§5.4). The caller writes that row; the function does no I/O besides its reads.

A clearance for a segment or profile write carries the [NetworkWriteClearance] the repositories demand, so those writes cannot reach the datastore without this function at all (a compile error, not a review finding). The other writes go through repository methods older than this module and shared with the seeder; network_gate_enumeration_test in axiam-api-rest pins every call site of them to a function that calls this one first, or to a named exemption with its reason.

A network-sensitive clearance of a role or binding write also carries what the decision read — GateClearance::role (the role’s name and its network: grants), GateClearance::node, GateClearance::permission, GateClearance::cleared_binding — so that the caller’s success row is built from what was decided on, not from reads after the write (R111W4-02).

§The one exemption from “you delegate only what you hold”

EXEMPTION_ORGANIZATION_BOOTSTRAP: an organization-level writer may assign a tenant’s own seeded super-admin role, tenant-wide, to a user of that tenant, although it does not itself hold the role’s network: permissions. Since #702 the organization scope’s super-admin holds none, so without this nobody could give a new tenant its first administrator — the documented bootstrap (docs/admin/organization-scope.md), and the intent of the review that asked for #702 (“each tenant’s own super-admin keeps the bootstrap”, R110W6-05, T-483). The bound, all of it checked here:

  • the writer’s SubjectScope is Organization (the REST extractor already confines the target tenant to that organization);
  • the role is the one the seeder makes: named [SEEDED_SUPER_ADMIN_ROLE], global, and carrying no network: grant but allows of [SEEDED_SUPER_ADMIN_NETWORK_ACTIONS] — a shape only the seeder can produce, since §4.2 rule 1 refuses a network: grant to any global role through the API;
  • the grantee is a user, the assignment names no resource, and the grantee is not the writer (H6’s reading: no self-configuration);
  • nothing else widens: a custom role carrying network: grants, a network: permission grant, a resource-scoped assignment and every other write stay under the §5.3 bound, and the organization scope’s own role still holds no network: permission. The cleared write is network-sensitive and leaves its success row, which names the exemption.

§What it is not

It is a namespace-limited delegation bound. Outside network:, roles:assign stays unbounded and so does certificates:bind for an account that holds no network:join (the note’s residual 7). “Holds” is a write-time condition: a profile or an assignment does not lapse when its writer later loses the permission (residual 1).

The route-level permission (roles:assign, permissions:grant, certificates:bind, …) is the caller’s to check first; this function adds to it and never replaces it.
