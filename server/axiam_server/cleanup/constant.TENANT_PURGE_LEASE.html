How long a replica’s claim on a tombstoned tenant’s purge keeps the other replicas off it (#610); a claim older than this is taken over.

The purge is not batched: it is one DELETE … WHERE tenant_id = $id per tenant-scoped table, about seventy statements in sequence, each removing all of the tenant’s rows of its table at once (the certificate tables three statements each), so even a large tenant’s purge is expected to take minutes; an hour leaves an order of magnitude of headroom before a purge still running could be overlapped — and an overlap is safe in any case: the steps are idempotent and only one purge’s final DELETE finds the tenant row, so the purge is recorded once. What the lease costs is the delay after a replica dies holding a claim: the tenant waits at most an hour for another replica, an erasure of data already out of every read path. Clock skew between replicas, which the stamp is written by, is seconds against an hour. A purge that does not finish releases its claim — one that fails, one given up because the process is stopping, and (#655) one aborted or panicking, from a drop guard — so the next tick, or the next start, resumes it at once; only a process that ends before the release is written leaves the tenant to the lease: a SIGKILL, or a unit cut at the stop’s bound, whose guard releases the claim from a spawned task that the process exit may cancel (#687).
